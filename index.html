Method

How the math works

Every output traces back to these formulas.

TargetPaid inbound target = Total target × Paid inbound share
From open pipeline = Open SQL pipeline × SQL→Won
New deals = (Paid inbound target − From open pipeline) ÷ ACV

Open pipeline counts toward the target first. Paid inbound volume is sized only for what is left.

Selling windowSelling window = Planning window − Sales cycle

Leads created after that point close outside the period, so the volume has to come in fewer months.

Funnel volumeSQLs = Deals ÷ SQL→Won
MQLs = SQLs ÷ MQL→SQL
Leads = MQLs ÷ Lead→MQL
Visits = Leads ÷ Visit→Lead
Spend and costPaid spend = Visits × Cost per visit
Actual CPL = Cost per visit ÷ Visit→Lead
Actual paid CAC = Actual CPL ÷ Lead→Won
CeilingsMax CAC = ACV × Gross margin × (Payback ÷ 12)
Max paid CAC = Max CAC × Paid share of CAC
Max CPL = Max paid CAC × Lead→Won

ACV is annual, so payback is measured against first-year gross profit. CPL and paid CAC test the same constraint.

PipelinePipeline needed for full target = Paid inbound target ÷ SQL→Won
New SQL pipeline = New SQLs × ACV
Coverage ratio = 1 ÷ SQL→Won
Model type. A deterministic planning model: fixed rates run through accounting identities. It estimates nothing from data and gives no confidence range. The three scenarios show sensitivity to conversion rates, not probability.

Assumptions. Conversion rates are period averages. Every new lead moves through the full sales cycle. Open pipeline closes inside the planning window at the SQL to closed-won rate. Cost per visit is flat at any budget; real auction costs rise as spend scales, so high-volume scenarios understate the true cost. Spend is spread evenly across the selling window. Whole deals and SQLs are shown rounded up; every calculation uses the exact values.

Default values are example inputs. Replace them with the client's historical data before drawing conclusions.
